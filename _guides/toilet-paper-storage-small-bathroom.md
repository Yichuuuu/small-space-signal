<p class="lede">A small bathroom needs a reachable spare roll without giving up the sink counter. Start by deciding how much paper belongs in the room: one backup beside the roll in use, a few covered spares, or a larger supply that frees a cupboard shelf. Each option below uses a different kind of space and requires no wall drilling.</p>

<h2 id="choose-the-location">Choose the location before the holder</h2>

<ul>
  <li><a href="#mdesign-tank-holder">Accessible tank, little floor space:</a> hang two rolls beside the tank if its rim and lid can accommodate a bracket.</li>
  <li><a href="#idesign-kent-canister">A small clear patch of floor:</a> a lidded canister keeps three backup rolls out of view.</li>
  <li><a href="#yamazaki-plate-stocker">A narrow but longer gap:</a> a slim steel stocker holds more rolls and dispenses them through a lower opening.</li>
</ul>

<p>Measure with a new roll of the paper you normally buy. Its diameter, width and the space needed to slide it out matter more than labels such as "mega." Check the bathroom door swing, flush handle and cleaning access too. Container dimensions describe the outside; leave extra room for your hand and any lid that needs lifting.</p>

<h2 id="refill-routine">Keep the refill routine simple</h2>

<p>Keep one reachable backup and store the rest of a bulk pack where it already fits. Add a larger holder only if moving the reserve into the bathroom frees useful space elsewhere. When cleaning, move freestanding storage and let the floor dry before putting it back. Check the supply at the same time so an opaque container does not hide an empty reserve.</p>
