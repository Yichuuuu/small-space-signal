<p class="lede">An unfinished puzzle can take over the only dining table in an apartment. A useful storage system lets you stop for dinner without dismantling the work, and has a home after it leaves the table. Start by choosing between a roll-up mat and a flat case. Sorting trays address a separate problem: loose pieces spread across every remaining surface.</p>

<p>The mat and case are alternatives, not a combined kit. The case already includes sorting compartments, so separate trays may be unnecessary. A tray or shallow box you already own may also be enough for loose pieces. Buy only the part that makes clearing and reopening the table easier.</p>

<h2 id="measure-puzzle-table-storage">Measure the puzzle, the table, and the storage space</h2>

<p>Read the finished dimensions printed on your puzzle box. A “1,000-piece” label does not establish its width and length: a wide panoramic puzzle and a conventional rectangle can need very different surfaces. Compare both dimensions with the assembly surface, leaving room to work around the edges.</p>

<p>Next, lay out the proposed mat or case footprint on the table with sheets of paper. Include somewhere to put the picture box and the sorting trays you actually use. A product that fits the tabletop but leaves no room for your hands can still be awkward. Support the whole working surface rather than letting a loaded board hang over a narrow table.</p>

<p>Finally, choose its storage home before purchasing. Check the opening, interior depth, and the route to it. Bed legs, sofa supports, closet doors, and shelf lips can block a case even when the visible gap looks large enough. A rolled mat also needs clearance and support; it does not disappear just because it is rolled.</p>

<h2 id="make-dinner-the-reset-point">Make dinner the reset point</h2>

<ol>
  <li><strong>Before starting:</strong> Put the assembly surface down first and keep its storage home empty. Bring out only the sorting groups you need.</li>
  <li><strong>Before moving:</strong> Return loose pieces to their trays, check the edges and closures, and secure the mat or case according to its directions.</li>
  <li><strong>Before dinner:</strong> Move everything to the planned location, check for stray pieces, and clean the table for food.</li>
  <li><strong>Next session:</strong> Reopen on a fully supported surface and check the puzzle before continuing. Adjust the routine if clearing away takes more effort than the storage saves.</li>
</ol>
