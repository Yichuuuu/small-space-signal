<p class="lede">In a small kitchen, a dish rack competes with either the sink or the preparation counter. The useful question is not simply which rack is smallest: it is where wet dishes can sit without blocking your next task. These three removable designs suit different routines, from a rack that rolls away to a compact draining station that stays beside the sink.</p>

<h2 id="measure-before-ordering">Measure the working area before ordering</h2>

<p>For an over-sink rack, measure the opening in the direction the rack will bridge, then identify firm, level support at both ends. Overall rack length is not the same as a supported opening of that length. Check the faucet, handles, rounded corners, and any raised sink rim. A rack balanced at the edge is not a suitable fit.</p>

<p>For a countertop rack, mark out its footprint with paper, then add your tallest plate and widest pan to the plan. Check cabinet clearance and how you will lift items out. A drainage spout also needs a clear route into the basin; a raised sink lip or a gap between rack and sink can matter as much as the rack's width. None of these products requires a drilled wall mounting.</p>

<h2 id="dry-over-the-sink">1. Dry over the sink, then roll the rack away</h2>

<p>The Kraus option makes sense when keeping the counter clear matters more than keeping the whole sink opening free. Its flat surface suits items that can rest securely on it. Think through the washing sequence first: if a pan must soak underneath while dishes dry above, will you still be able to reach the faucet and drain?</p>

<h2 id="make-the-space-work-every-day">Make the space work every day</h2>

<p>Before ordering, walk through a complete small load: where dirty dishes wait, where you wash, where clean items dry, and where they go afterward. A rack that blocks the only clear chopping area or forces you to move wet dishes to use the tap may be the wrong design, even if it technically fits.</p>

<p>Wash in manageable batches, leave space between items, and put them away when dry. If the real problem is too many utensils around the basin, start with our <a href="{{ '/articles/small-kitchen-sink-picks.html' | relative_url }}">sink-area organization guide</a>. If dry cookware has nowhere to go, the next useful step may be <a href="{{ '/articles/small-kitchen-storage-picks.html' | relative_url }}">organizing the kitchen storage you already have</a>, not adding a larger drying rack.</p>
