<p class="lede">Trading cards can outgrow a desk long before they need a whole cabinet. Start with how you use the cards: grab a complete deck, browse individual favorites, or sort a reserve collection. A small shelf works better when its container matches that job.</p>

<p>These are three different storage jobs. Buy for the pile that currently lacks a home; an empty second container can become another reason to keep cards you no longer want. None requires wall mounting, so each can move with you when a rental's furniture layout changes.</p>

<h2 id="measure-the-shelf-and-cards">Measure the shelf and the protected card</h2>

<p>Measure clear shelf height, depth and width between supports. Then leave room for your fingers and for removing the container without tipping its neighbors. A case that fits while closed still needs somewhere to open: reserve a clear patch of desk for a binder or a long cardboard lid.</p>

<p>Measure cards in the sleeves or holders you actually use. A standard card in two sleeves, a thick sports card and a graded slab have different storage needs. A product's capacity number is not a universal promise. If the closure presses on cards or the pages bulge, reduce the load.</p>

<h2 id="keep-the-collection-contained">Give the collection a shelf limit</h2>

<p>Choose a visible stopping point: one shelf section or one container for each job you need. Label the outside with the deck, set or category so you can find it without opening everything. Keep an inventory note for cards that have moved between a deck and storage.</p>

<p>Re-sort before adding another container. If you keep rearranging the shelf just to reach a favorite deck, move that deck to the easiest position and put less-used cards farther back.
