A stepped riser, an undershelf rack, and a set of clips address those problems differently. None creates extra cabinet volume, and you do not need to buy all three.</p>

<p>Empty one shelf and group what you actually keep there before measuring. Use the clear space inside the hinges and door frame, not the cabinet's outside width. Leave room to lift a can or pull a box out. Keep heavy items on a suitably rated, easy-to-reach shelf; unused air below a high shelf is not automatically a good place for more weight.</p>

<h2 id="close-bags-without-decanting-everything">3. Close the bags you already use</h2>

<p>Clips are worth considering when an existing bag already fits your pantry, but its opening gets in the way. They let you keep the original label and package without adding a separate container for every item. Start by checking whether the original closure still works; a new clip should solve a specific problem.</p>

<h2 id="avoid-crowding-the-cabinet-again">Avoid crowding the cabinet again</h2>

<p>Make one change, put your usual groceries back, and check the reach to every row. Do not place the can riser directly beneath the undershelf rack unless the cans and your hand still have enough clearance. Leave a visible home for spare clips so they do not become drawer clutter.</p>
