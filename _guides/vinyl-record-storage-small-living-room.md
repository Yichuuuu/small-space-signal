<p class="lede">Put a record on and see where the empty jacket lands. If it takes the last seat on the sofa, a small display stand may be all you need. If choosing the next album means moving a whole stack, measure for a crate or case. All three options below work without fastening anything to the wall.</p>

<h2 id="choose-your-storage">Where do your records go between plays?</h2>

<ul>
  <li><a href="#big-fudge-stand">Beside the turntable.</a> Give the jacket from the record you are playing a spot of its own.</li>
  <li><a href="#crosley-crate">Within reach of the sofa.</a> Keep an open row of albums you can browse by cover.</li>
  <li><a href="#victrola-collector">Back in a cupboard.</a> Close the lid and carry a smaller selection off the shared table in one trip.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="check-your-shelf">Check the shelf you already own</h2>

<p>Stand a few sleeved albums upright on the shelf you have in mind. Can you read the spines, take one out, and put it back while the others stay supported? If so, the shelf can hold the collection and you can reserve the space beside the player for the current jacket. The Library of Congress recommends storing discs upright on edge. <a href="https://www.loc.gov/preservation/care/record.html">Library of Congress storage guidance</a>.</p>
