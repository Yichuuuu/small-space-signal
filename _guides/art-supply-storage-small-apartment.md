<p class="lede">When your dining table doubles as an art desk, storage needs to make clearing up easy. A useful starting point is one container for the tools you actually use in a session, with a measured place to put it afterward. A pencil case, a brush holder and a divided box solve different parts of that problem.</p>

<h2 id="choose-by-tools">Choose around your working kit</h2>

<ul>
  <li><a href="#lihit-lab-book-pen-case">A few pencils, pens and an eraser:</a> a small case that opens flat keeps a daily sketch kit together.</li>
  <li><a href="#artbin-brush-box">Paintbrushes with delicate tips:</a> a shallow box with foam holders separates them from loose tools.</li>
  <li><a href="#artbin-large-solutions-box">Several groups of small supplies:</a> adjustable dividers give erasers, sharpeners and capped tubes their own sections.</li>
</ul>

<p>Measure your longest tool and the usable drawer opening, including the space past hinges or drawer stops. The dimensions below describe the containers' exteriors. They do not guarantee that a tool of the same length will fit inside. You can start with just the option that matches your main activity.</p>

<h2 id="clear-the-table">Give the kit a place to return to</h2>

<p>Reserve a drawer or shelf before buying storage. Put the kit you use most where you can lift it out in one move, and store spare supplies separately. Return each tool as you finish with it. Paper and unfinished work need their own flat place so containers do not press marks into them.</p>
