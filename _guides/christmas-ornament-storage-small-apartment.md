<p class="lede">A box can fit beneath the mattress and still catch on the bed rail. Measure from the floor or rug to the lowest point the box must pass. For a closet cubby, measure the opening inside its front lip. Then lay your widest ornament on a sheet of paper and trace it, including its cap and any projecting wings or branches. Those measurements tell you which of these three boxes can hold your decorations and still come out easily next year.</p>

<h2 id="choose-an-ornament-box">Which box fits the space you have?</h2>

<ul>
  <li><a href="#sterilite-adjustable-case">A low bed rail and ornaments in several shapes.</a> A shallow, single-layer case lets you lift one piece out without unpacking the others.</li>
  <li><a href="#holdn-underbed-trays">A bed with more than 6 inches of clearance and a set of 3-inch balls.</a> Two lift-out trays let you carry half the collection to the tree at a time.</li>
  <li><a href="#simplify-ornament-cube">A closet cubby about a foot wide and a collection of small balls.</a> A cube uses shelf height and leaves the floor available.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="packing-a-fragile-ornament">What if one ornament won't fit a divider?</h2>

<p>For a glass bird with a long tail or a keepsake with a projecting hook, try its original fitted box first. Keep the whole box alongside the organizer if that lets the lid close without pressing on the ornament. Tape a short contents list to the end that faces the room so you can find it without unstacking the shelf.</p>
