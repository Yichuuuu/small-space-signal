<p>Measure the storage location separately from the work area. A board standing at the back of a counter still has to lie flat when used. A bottle that fits a shelf needs space around it to grip and remove it. All three options work without drilling or a power outlet, but none creates more counter space by itself. Keep a board, spoon, or mixing jar you already find convenient.</p>

<h2 id="upright-boards-for-a-repeatable-reset">1. Upright boards for a repeatable reset</h2>

<p>Consider a nesting set if several loose boards are the part of your kitchen that never seems to have a home. For a salad, choose a board for the ingredients in front of you, transfer them to your bowl, then clear that work area before dressing the salad. If you only need one small board, buying three may add storage rather than solve it.</p>

<h2 id="a-small-counter-workflow">A small-counter workflow</h2>

<ol>
  <li><strong>Choose one work zone.</strong> Put the serving bowl within reach, leaving a clear, stable area for the board. Avoid crowding all the ingredients and tools onto it at once.</li>
  <li><strong>Transfer before the next task.</strong> Move prepared ingredients to the bowl and clear scraps. Bring out the avocado tool only if today's meal calls for it.</li>
  <li><strong>Mix the amount you need.</strong> Use a jar or shaker for dressing, then pour over the portion you are serving. A smaller vessel is not a reason to prepare extra.</li>
  <li><strong>Reset the storage spots.</strong> Clean and dry tools according to their care instructions before returning them. Check that the board set can stand securely and that the drawer closes without catching the slicer.</li>
</ol>
