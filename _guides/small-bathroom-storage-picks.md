<h2 id="countertop-storage">1. Contain daily items on the counter</h2>

<p>Try marking out a small square beside the basin. If it blocks the faucet handle or leaves no comfortable place to wash your face, countertop storage is not the right answer. Keep a caddy out of standing water and leave room to lift it when wiping the surface.</p>

<h2 id="wall-storage">2. Hang one lightweight item on a suitable surface</h2>

<p>A hook can free a chair back or counter edge, but the hanging item also needs clearance. Hold a towel in the proposed position first: it should not brush the toilet, block a cabinet, or trail on the floor. Wet towels can weigh more than the same towels when dry.</p>

<h2 id="floor-storage">3. Use a narrow floor space without blocking access</h2>

<p>Measure the gap at its narrowest point, including baseboards, pipework, and the toilet's widest section. Then check where the organizer will go when pulled out. A cart that fits only while tightly wedged in place will be frustrating to restock and move for cleaning.</p>

<h2 id="fit-and-care">A quick check before ordering</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Measure the usable space and the item you want to store, not just the organizer's outside dimensions.</li>
  <li>Match the selected model, color, size, and pack quantity to the option described here.</li>
  <li>Keep access to the sink, toilet, cabinet doors, and cleaning tools clear. Do not create a new obstacle to solve a storage problem.</li>
  <li>Check Amazon's current seller, price, delivery terms, returns, and availability before purchasing.</li>
</ul>
