<p class="lede">Look inside your flashlight and your most-used remote before giving batteries a whole drawer. A flashlight that takes four AAs needs just four spare slots for its next change. If your remotes use AAAs and your lantern takes D cells, count those separately. An organizer can have dozens of empty slots and still be full in the size you need.</p>

<h2 id="measure-the-drawer">What will fit when the drawer closes?</h2>

<p>Measure the clear floor space beside the things you already keep there. Then measure from the drawer bottom to the lowest rail or frame it passes under. The drawer front can be taller than that opening.</p>

<ul>
  <li><a href="#storacell-aa-caddy">One spare set of AAs</a> fits in a small caddy beside the device that uses it.</li>
  <li><a href="#range-kleen-tray">Several sizes in one drawer</a> are easy to count in an open tray.</li>
  <li><a href="#battery-daddy-smart">Batteries you carry from room to room</a> can travel together in a latched case that lives on a shelf.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="store-the-spares">Where to keep the spares</h2>

<p>Choose a cool, dry spot. Keep loose keys and paper clips away from exposed battery terminals. For spare coin and button cells, keep the original child-resistant packaging closed until use and store it out of children’s reach. <a href="https://energizer.com/about-batteries/battery-care/">Energizer battery care</a>. <a href="https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-home/Pages/what-parents-need-to-know-about-button-and-lithium-coin-batteries.aspx">American Academy of Pediatrics guidance on coin and button cells</a>.</p>
