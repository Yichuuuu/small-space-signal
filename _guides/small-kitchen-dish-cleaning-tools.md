<h2 id="give-the-palm-brush-a-place">1. Give a short dish brush its own resting place</h2>

<p>A palm brush suits accessible surfaces such as plates and open pans. The short grip also keeps your hand close to the washing area; choose a longer-handled tool if that position does not suit your routine. The holder is the relevant space-management feature here: it gives the brush a defined destination instead of leaving it on the counter.</p>

<h2 id="check-bottle-opening-and-reach">2. Check a bottle brush against the bottle, not just the drawer</h2>

<p>A reusable bottle can be easy to store but awkward to clean around its shoulders and base. A long brush addresses that access problem. Before buying, compare the opening and interior shape with the brush head, and decide where the wet brush will hang or stand after use.</p>

<h2 id="measure-the-drain-seat">3. Match the strainer to the drain seat</h2>

<p>A removable basket can collect scraps without adding another countertop container. Its usefulness depends on whether it sits securely over the drain while allowing water through. Measure the recess and check for raised hardware before replacing a strainer that already works.</p>

<h2 id="leave-room-for-drying">Leave room for drying and cleanup</h2>

<p>Place the palm-brush holder on a supported counter area, reserve a separate hanging or upright position for the bottle brush, and keep the drain strainer accessible. We have not tested whether both brushes fit in one caddy or whether the bottle brush fits the palm-brush holder. Do not buy around an unverified combined fit.</p>

<p>After washing, empty captured scraps, rinse the tools according to their care directions, and give them somewhere to dry. Avoid packing wet brushes into a closed drawer just to make the counter look clear. Keep food-contact dish brushes separate from brushes used on bathroom surfaces or other non-food cleaning tasks.</p>

<h2 id="keep-the-sink-setup-small">Keep the setup as small as the job allows</h2>

<p>If only bottles are difficult to clean, start with the bottle brush. If the drain already has a well-fitting basket, keep it. If a palm brush would crowd the only preparation surface, its storage base is not a space-saving upgrade for that kitchen. The goal is a routine that is easy to put away, not three additional objects by the tap.</p>
