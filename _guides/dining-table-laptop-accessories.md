<p class="lede">Closing a laptop does not clear a dining-table desk if the mouse, adapters, and cables stay behind. A workable small-space setup needs two layouts: enough room to use your equipment, and a defined place to put it afterward. These three optional accessories address different parts of that transition. You do not need a hub for a Bluetooth mouse, and you do not need a new pouch if a bag you already own does the job.</p>

<p>Start by laying out only the accessories you use during an ordinary work session. Put occasional-use cables elsewhere. Then choose a shelf, drawer, or bag for the daily set. None of these products needs a wall mount, adhesive pad, or drilled hole, but buying portable gear without choosing its storage spot can simply move the clutter from the table to a chair.</p>

<h2 id="remove-one-loose-cable">1. Remove one loose cable, if you need a separate mouse</h2>

<p>A Bluetooth mouse makes sense when you prefer a separate pointer but want fewer connections to undo before dinner. If your laptop's trackpad already suits your work, keeping it is the smaller setup. A low-profile mouse also does not remove the need for a clear movement area beside the laptop.</p>

<h2 id="group-only-the-usb-accessories-you-use">2. Group only the USB-A accessories you actually use</h2>

<p>A small data hub can turn several separate laptop connections into one upstream cable. That is useful for a wired keyboard, a receiver you already own, or an occasional USB drive. It does not make the attached cables disappear. Keep the hub on the table with some slack in its lead instead of letting connected accessories pull it over the edge.</p>

<h2 id="give-small-gear-one-storage-home">3. Give small gear one storage home</h2>

<p>A two-compartment pouch is most useful when it becomes the daily home for loose accessories, not an overflow bag for every spare cable. Use one compartment for the larger disconnected items and the other for small leads or adapters. Keep only the pieces needed for the next session in the daily set; that makes packing and checking for a missing cable easier.</p>

<h2 id="make-clearing-the-table-repeatable">Make clearing the table repeatable</h2>

<ol>
  <li><strong>Finish data transfers first.</strong> Save your work and safely eject any removable storage before disconnecting a hub.</li>
  <li><strong>Separate storage from charging.</strong> Disconnect accessories before packing; leave any device that still needs charging in an appropriate open location.</li>
  <li><strong>Pack the daily set in the same order.</strong> Switch off the mouse, loosely coil leads, then put each item in its assigned compartment. A bulging pouch is a signal to reduce the set, not force the zipper.</li>
  <li><strong>Put the set in its chosen home.</strong> Return it to the same drawer, shelf, or bag so the table can be used for meals without moving a second pile.</li>
</ol>
