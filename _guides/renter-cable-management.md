<p class="lede">Cable clutter is usually a routing problem before it is an accessory problem. Identify where power enters, which devices move, and what must stay accessible during a reset.</p>

<h2>Sort cables by behavior</h2>

<p>Make three groups. Fixed cables connect equipment that rarely moves, such as a monitor or desktop computer. Service cables must be unplugged occasionally for charging, travel, or troubleshooting. Data and audio cables may be sensitive to tight bends or interference. Giving each group its own route makes later changes much easier.</p>

<p>Remove obsolete cables before organizing. Trace both ends while equipment is off where appropriate, label uncertain connections, and follow device instructions. Do not disconnect network, medical, security, or building equipment you are not authorized to alter.</p>

<h2>Place power safely</h2>

<p>Use only listed power strips and extension products appropriate for the load and location. Do not daisy-chain power strips or conceal power equipment where heat cannot escape. The power strip’s switch and plug should remain reachable. Cable management should never defeat a product’s ventilation instructions or create a trip hazard.</p>

<p>Mounting a power strip beneath a desk can reduce hanging weight, but use the strip’s intended mounting method. Adhesive strength varies by surface, temperature, and load. The combined pull of several stiff cords can exceed what the strip itself appears to weigh.</p>

<h2>Create a service loop</h2>

<p>Devices that move need a small reserve of cable. Secure the route before the loop so a tug does not reach the connector. Keep the loop broad rather than sharply folded, and follow minimum bend guidance for fiber, display, and other specialized cables.</p>

<p>At the desk edge, a removable clip can keep a charging cable from falling. Choose an opening matched to the cable diameter and a base wide enough to resist sideways pull. Clips that pinch the cable jacket are not a good fit.</p>

<h2>Test before committing adhesive</h2>

<p>Use painter’s tape to mock up routes for several days. Sit, stand, rotate the monitor, open drawers, and move the chair fully. Confirm that no cable is tensioned at the extremes. Once the route works, clean the surface and install the chosen clips exactly as instructed.</p>

<p>Avoid placing adhesive on fragile finishes, wallpaper, unknown coatings, or surfaces excluded by the manufacturer. Keep the removal tabs visible. Photograph the intended removal direction before the desk hides it.</p>

<aside class="decision-box">
<h3>Final inspection</h3>

<ul><li>Power connections are ventilated and reachable.</li><li>No cable crosses a sharp edge or moving joint.</li><li>Frequently unplugged cables have a service loop.</li><li>Labels can be read without dismantling the setup.</li><li>The desk and chair can complete their full range of motion.</li></ul>
</aside>

<h2>Optimize for the next change</h2>

<p>A system is successful when one failed charger can be replaced without cutting ten ties. Use reversible fasteners, separate fixed and mobile cables, and leave small sections visible where identification matters. Perfect concealment is less valuable than safe, understandable routing.</p>
