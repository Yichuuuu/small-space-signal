<h2 id="start-with-permitted-work">Start with permitted work</h2>

<p>A toolkit does not replace the lease, building rules, or qualified trades. Confirm what installation and repair work is allowed. Do not alter electrical, plumbing, gas, structural, fire-safety, or security systems unless authorized and qualified to do so.</p>

<p>Before moving belongings in, document existing conditions according to the property’s process. Date-stamped photos, written notes, and required inspection forms are more useful than attempting unauthorized repairs.</p>

<h2 id="measurement-and-documentation">Measurement and documentation</h2>

<p>Carry a reliable tape measure long enough for the largest room dimension, a small level, pencil, removable labels, and a phone charger. Measure doorways, elevators, stairs, and turns before moving large furniture. Record the narrowest dimension and account for handles, trim, and the diagonal path of the item.</p>

<p>A simple notebook or offline phone note should hold access instructions, utility details, furniture hardware counts, and critical dimensions. Keep personal access codes private and do not leave them on box labels.</p>

<h2 id="assembly-basics">Assembly basics</h2>

<p>A multi-bit screwdriver, hex-key set in common sizes, adjustable wrench, and small pliers cover many ordinary furniture tasks. Use the tool specified by the furniture manufacturer; the wrong bit can strip fasteners. A compact parts tray or resealable bags keep hardware grouped by item.</p>

<p>A lightweight utility knife is useful for boxes, but it must be carried and stored safely. Cut away from the body, retract the blade immediately, and protect contents by keeping the blade shallow. Scissors are often safer for soft packing materials.</p>

<h2 id="protect-the-route">Protect the route</h2>

<p>Use floor protection approved for the surface, and keep it flat so it does not become a trip hazard. Furniture sliders must match both the floor and the item weight. Lift with enough people and suitable equipment rather than relying on a small hand tool to compensate for an unsafe load.</p>

<p>Inspect the path for wet areas, loose rugs, low fixtures, and tight corners. Keep children and pets away from the moving route. Building requirements for elevator padding or time reservations should be handled before the truck arrives.</p>

<h2 id="add-a-first-night-module">Add a first-night module</h2>

<p>Include a flashlight, batteries compatible with it, basic personal protective equipment, charging cables, paper towels, trash bags, and only the cleaning supplies that can be transported and stored safely. Keep medication, water, food, documents, and personal essentials separate from the tool kit.</p>

<p>A small pack of manufacturer-approved furniture pads or removable labels can help organize immediately. Do not apply adhesive to an unfamiliar finish without checking compatibility.</p>

<h2 id="keep-the-kit-after-the-move">Keep the kit after the move</h2>

<p>Replace consumed items, remove duplicate tools, and store the kit somewhere accessible. It becomes a compact household maintenance kit and will already be organized for the next move. The goal is not a complete workshop; it is a dependable set for predictable, permitted tasks.</p>
