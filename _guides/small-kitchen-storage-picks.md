<h2 id="narrow-cutlery-storage">1. Give everyday cutlery a narrower home</h2>

<p>An angled tray trades drawer width for vertical clearance. That can leave space beside the cutlery for another category, but only if the drawer still closes with your own utensils in place. It is not a useful trade in a very shallow drawer.</p>

<h2 id="container-lid-storage">2. File container lids upright</h2>

<p>A lid organizer is most useful after you have matched lids to containers. Give each family of lids a section, with the sizes you use most often closest to the cabinet opening. Avoid filling every gap so tightly that removing one lid pulls out several.</p>

<h2 id="pan-and-lid-storage">3. Separate pans and pot lids instead of stacking</h2>

<p>Vertical storage can make one pan easier to retrieve without unstacking the rest. It also needs more uninterrupted cabinet height. Before buying a rack, stand your intended items in their proposed orientation and check the cabinet door, the shelf above, and protruding handles.</p>

<h2 id="before-ordering">Before ordering</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Measure the opening, the usable interior, and the loaded height. Make a paper footprint if the space is close.</li>
  <li>Match the selected size, color, and model to the version described here. Variations can have different dimensions.</li>
  <li>Check the current seller, delivery terms, returns, price, and availability on Amazon. Some versions may show buying options from other sellers rather than a featured offer.</li>
  <li>Choose the one organizer that solves a problem you actually have. A clear zone that works without a purchase is a good result too.</li>
</ul>
