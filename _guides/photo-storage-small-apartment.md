<p class="lede">A 5 × 7 school portrait can decide where a whole envelope of 4 × 6 snapshots will fit. Take out your largest print, including its white border, and measure both edges. Then think about the last time you went looking for a picture. Did you want to pass an album around, take out one vacation, or find a particular year?</p>

<h2 id="find-a-photo">Choose by how you find a photo</h2>

<ul>
  <li><a href="#itoya-profolio">For pictures you show people</a>, a pocket album keeps a small selection in viewing order.</li>
  <li><a href="#iris-photo-keeper">For sets you take out together</a>, six separate cases let you lift one event out of the cupboard.</li>
  <li><a href="#pioneer-photo-box">For loose prints you file by date</a>, dividers let a busy year take more space than a quiet one.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="pick-a-shelf">Find a shelf before moving the prints</h2>

<p>A cool, dry closet shelf is a better home for family photos than a damp basement storage locker. If you already have a box there that fits your largest print, try dating the dividers and retrieving one event before buying more storage. <a href="https://www.archives.gov/publications/prologue/2015/fall/handling-photos.html">National Archives guidance on storing family photos</a>.</p>
