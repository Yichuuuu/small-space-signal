<h2 id="bring-a-small-group-forward">1. Bring a small group forward</h2>

<p>A rolling bin is useful when you repeatedly move several items to reach one at the back. Group a few individually closed condiment jars, snack packs, or other ingredients you use together. It is less useful if every container already sits in a visible single row; the bin's walls would then consume space without solving a reach problem.</p>

<h2 id="cover-cut-produce-without-a-large-box">2. Cover cut produce without a large box</h2>

<p>If you often use half a lemon or part of a cucumber, a flexible cover can avoid dedicating a much larger rigid box to that one piece. The useful comparison is with your existing storage habit, not an assumption that silicone is always the better choice. Keep using a small reusable container if it fits well and makes the food easy to find.</p>

<h2 id="reserve-height-for-an-herb-keeper">3. Reserve height for an herb keeper</h2>

<p>An upright herb container deserves space when you regularly bring home bunches that otherwise disappear behind larger items. It is a more substantial object than a silicone cover. If you only buy a few sprigs occasionally, or cannot spare nearly ten inches of closed-container height, this particular keeper is a poor fit for your refrigerator.</p>

<h2 id="keep-the-system-smaller-than-the-problem">Keep the system smaller than the problem</h2>

<ol>
  <li><strong>Check what is already there.</strong> Before shopping, identify the ingredients you regularly lose sight of. One recurring problem is enough to start with.</li>
  <li><strong>Measure usable space.</strong> Account for shelf edges, the door's swing, handles, and the height of loaded bottles. Outside dimensions are not usable interior dimensions.</li>
  <li><strong>Keep labels readable.</strong> Grouping food should not hide package dates or turn the back of a bin into another forgotten corner.</li>
  <li><strong>Reassess before adding more.</strong> If a storage piece makes an ingredient harder to reach or clean up, simplify the arrangement. Organizers do not replace appropriate refrigeration or food-specific storage guidance.</li>
</ol>
