<h2 id="match-the-tool-to-the-meal">Match the tool to the meal</h2>

<p>Think about both the sink and the storage space. A tool that folds thinner still needs its full length and width somewhere. Leave room to lift it out without catching it on a drawer divider, and consider where wet equipment will dry before it goes away. If you already have a suitable colander or masher, keeping it may save more space than adding a second version.</p>

<p>A brush is not a peeler, a colander does not hold water like a bowl, and a masher is not a blender. Pick the tools that match what you actually cook rather than treating the table above as a required shopping list. None needs a permanent mounting point or a dedicated appliance space.</p>

<h2 id="keep-a-small-brush-for-produce">1. Keep a small brush for produce</h2>

<p>A compact produce brush makes sense if firm-skinned vegetables are a regular part of your meals. Give it a recognizable spot so it does not get mixed up with brushes used for dishes or household cleaning. Its small body is useful in a crowded sink area, but it still needs somewhere to drain and dry.</p>

<h2 id="collapse-the-colander-between-meals">2. Collapse the colander between meals</h2>

<p>A rigid colander can occupy a large bowl-shaped space even when empty. A collapsible version can reduce that stored bulk while leaving you a separate tool for rinsing or draining. The useful comparison is with your current cabinet arrangement: if an existing colander already nests neatly inside a bowl, replacing it may not improve much.</p>

<h2 id="fold-the-masher-head-for-storage">3. Fold the masher head for storage</h2>

<p>A conventional masher can snag other utensils because its head stays at right angles to the handle. Delta changes that shape for storage. It is most relevant if you regularly mash cooked potatoes or similar soft food and the fixed head of your current tool makes the drawer difficult to close.</p>

<h2 id="put-dry-tools-back-in-their-own-spaces">Put dry tools back in their own spaces</h2>

<p>Let washed equipment dry, then collapse the empty colander and fold the clean masher. Give the produce brush its own drying location. Do not assume the three items nest together or that a container shown elsewhere on the site fits them. A flat cabinet space for the colander and an accessible utensil drawer may be more practical than buying one more organizer.</p>
