<h2 id="choose-by-the-container">Choose by the container, not the word “opener”</h2>

<p>A jar gripper cannot cut a metal can lid. A pointed can punch makes an opening for pouring; it does not cut around the entire lid. Separating these jobs helps avoid buying a tool that is small enough for the drawer but wrong for dinner.</p>

<h2 id="allow-room-for-the-can-opener">1. Give the can opener enough drawer clearance</h2>

<p>If canned beans, soup, or tomatoes are regular ingredients, a full-lid opener addresses a frequent task. A handheld version keeps the counter clear between meals. Its thickness matters as much as its length: an organizer that raises the tool above the drawer bottom uses some of the available closing clearance.</p>

<h2 id="keep-the-jar-pad-with-the-tool">2. Keep a jar gripper and its pad together</h2>

<p>For sauce jars and other screw-top containers, the problem may be getting purchase on the lid rather than cutting anything. A loose, handheld tool avoids installing an under-cabinet opener. Its separate base pad needs an assigned place too, or the useful half of the setup can disappear beneath other utensils.</p>

<h2 id="separate-cap-lifting-from-lid-cutting">3. Separate cap lifting from full-lid cutting</h2>

<p>A narrow bottle opener makes sense if you regularly open soda or other drinks with metal pop-off caps. The other end of this model is a can punch, so consider whether that second function is useful in your kitchen. If an existing tool already handles your bottles, a second opener adds duplication rather than saving space.</p>

<h2 id="plan-one-accessible-drawer-zone">Plan one accessible drawer zone</h2>

<p>Measure the drawer's usable length and width, then check the height between its base and anything it passes beneath when closing. Account for an existing tray, liner, and neighboring handles. A paper rectangle matching a tool's listed footprint can help plan the layout, but it cannot test the tool's thickness or moving parts.</p>

<p>Keep the opening tools you use frequently near the preparation area, with enough space to pick one up without reaching across a cutting wheel or pointed end. Store the jar pad in the same zone, not damp underneath a pile of metal tools. A larger organizer is unnecessary if an existing compartment already keeps the items separated and accessible.</p>

<h2 id="buy-for-the-missing-function">Buy for the missing function</h2>

<p>Choose the side-cut opener when you need to remove whole food-can lids, the jar opener when lid grip is the recurring issue, and the slim bottle tool when pop-off caps or a can-punch function are missing. These are three separate purchases, not a matched set or a recommendation to buy all three. None requires a permanent countertop location.</p>
