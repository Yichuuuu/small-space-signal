<p class="lede">Game night can leave a small dining table covered in empty boxes, cards and piles of tokens. Choosing a storage case starts with the contents of one game. Fold the board, gather the rulebook and put the pieces beside them, then measure the shelf or drawer where everything will go.</p>

<h2 id="choose-by-components">Choose by the pieces you need to store</h2>

<ul>
  <li><a href="#artbin-super-satchel-slim">A flat board and a few bagged pieces.</a> A shallow, undivided case can use a low shelf, provided the folded board fits.</li>
  <li><a href="#gamegenic-token-silo">Lots of small tokens.</a> Removable trays can travel from the storage box to each player's place at the table.</li>
  <li><a href="#bcw-hinged-card-box">A compact deck.</a> A small hinged box can keep one game's cards together in a drawer.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="try-one-game">Try the arrangement with one game</h2>

<p>Pack a single game, set it in its intended spot and take it out again as if you were starting a session. Check whether the label is visible, whether the lid opens easily and whether every component returns to the same place. Keep the rules and a component list with the game. A game-specific insert can be worth its shelf space when it keeps setup quick.</p>
