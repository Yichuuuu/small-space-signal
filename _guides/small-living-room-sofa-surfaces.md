These three options use different supports: a C-shaped table stands on the floor, an arm tray clips to compatible upholstery, and a lap desk rests on your legs. They are alternatives for different situations, not a set you need to buy together.</p>

<h2 id="choose-where-the-surface-sits">Choose where the surface can actually sit</h2>

<p>Start with the room while someone is sitting in the usual spot. Check the walkway, the sofa's lower frame, the arm's width and shape, and where you could put a portable surface before standing up. A small tabletop does not help if its base blocks your feet or you have nowhere to set it down. Also measure the object you want to use on it, rather than relying on a laptop's screen size.</p>

<h2 id="use-a-small-floor-standing-table">1. Use a small floor-standing table</h2>

<p>A C-shaped table is the option to consider if you want a surface that stays in place when you stand up. Its open side may let the tabletop sit closer to the seat, provided the base can enter the space below the furniture. This is not automatic: a low sofa frame, a thick rug, or a tall cushion can prevent the position you had in mind.</p>

<h2 id="check-the-sofa-arm-shape">2. Check the sofa arm before choosing a clip-on tray</h2>

<p>An arm tray can avoid adding another base on the floor, but it depends on the furniture for support. Arm width alone is not enough: shape, softness, seams, and the way the upholstery compresses affect how the tray sits. Treat the listed ranges as screening measurements, then check the particular arm and the supplied instructions.</p>

<h2 id="use-a-portable-lap-surface">3. Use a portable lap surface for a short session</h2>

<p>A lap desk suits someone who wants a firmer place for a laptop or notebook without leaving another table in the room. It moves with you and needs a parking spot before you stand. It is not a floor-standing table or a height-adjustable workstation, so consider how it feels in the seat you actually use rather than treating portability as a substitute for fit.</p>

<h2 id="make-space-before-ordering">Make space before ordering</h2>

<ul>

  <li>Check occupied-seat clearance, floor obstacles, arm shape, and any moving recliner parts.</li>
  <li>Decide where the surface will live when unused; keep the walking route clear.</li>
