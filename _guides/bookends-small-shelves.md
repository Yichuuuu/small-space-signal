<p class="lede">A few books on a desk or shallow shelf can become a leaning pile every time you pull one out. Before adding another piece of furniture, give that row a defined place. These three options support closed books in different ways and sit on an existing surface without wall mounting.</p>

<h2 id="choose-the-support">Choose the support that matches the problem</h2>

<ul>
  <li><a href="#officemate-adjustable-93190">Officemate Adjustable Bookends:</a> for a row whose width changes as notebooks and reference books come and go.</li>
  <li><a href="#yamazaki-tower-6872">Yamazaki Tower Bookends:</a> for books that share a desk with reading glasses, pens or other small essentials.</li>
  <li><a href="#lihit-lab-a3580">LIHIT LAB A4 Medium Book Stand:</a> for a narrow group of books you want to remove individually, with moving stoppers helping support the neighbors.</li>
</ul>

<p>Choose one approach for one shelf section. Matching the support to a real retrieval problem is more useful than buying enough organizers for every book you might own later. Keep frequently used books near the front and leave an open patch of desk where you can read them.</p>

<h2 id="measure-books-and-clearance">Measure the books as well as the shelf</h2>

<p>Measure usable shelf depth, clear height and the width you can spare. Include the tallest book, any part of the organizer that moves upward, and room for your hand. The height of a bookend is not the maximum height of a book, and the base dimensions do not guarantee that an oversized book will sit securely.</p>

<p>Line up the books you intend to store and measure their combined spine widths. Include folders and covers in that measurement. A short row of thick binders can use more width than several novels. Keep the organizer fully on a stable, level surface; do not let a base overhang the shelf edge to make it fit.</p>

<h2 id="make-the-row-easy-to-use">Make the row easy to use</h2>

<p>Start with a small working collection. Group books by when you reach for them, then leave the dividers or pocket accessible. Pull out a frequently used book and check whether you can return it without shifting everything else. If the row keeps leaning or catching, reduce the load and reconsider the fit.</p>
