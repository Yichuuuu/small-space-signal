<p class="lede">A compact living room can feel crowded long before it runs out of furniture. Loose accessories occupy the table, a spare throw has nowhere to go, and the sofa-side corner becomes a pile. These three freestanding options address different problems: use an existing surface, hide soft items inside a footrest, or combine a small table with open storage. You do not need all three.</p>

<h2 id="choose-living-room-job">Choose the clutter problem before adding furniture</h2>

<p>First check whether an existing tray or drawer can do the job. If you need something new, mark its footprint with removable paper on the floor or tabletop. Sit down, reach for a drink, open nearby doors, and walk past it. Storage only helps when returning an item is easy and the route through the room stays clear.</p>

<h2 id="gather-small-accessories">1. Gather everyday accessories on an existing surface</h2>

<p>If the coffee table is already the right size, start with a small organizer instead of another piece of furniture. Assign each section a job: glasses or a phone upright, small loose items in the tray. Try your actual items for fit; an organizer's outside measurements are not its usable compartment dimensions.</p>

<h2 id="hide-a-spare-throw">2. Give a spare throw a home inside a folding footrest</h2>

<p>A storage ottoman makes sense when you already want a footrest. It is less useful if it sits in the walkway or holds something you need every few minutes: each retrieval means clearing and lifting the lid. Compare its height with your sofa seat, and do not assume a 15-inch exterior means a 15-inch storage cavity.</p>

<h2 id="combine-table-and-basket">3. Combine a sofa-side surface with open storage</h2>

<p>If there is no suitable place for a drink or book, a basket table can combine two functions in one footprint. Check both the tabletop height and the reach from your seat. A round top still needs its full diameter of clear floor space; leave the basket opening accessible rather than wedging the table tightly between furniture.</p>

<h2 id="keep-the-walkway-open">Keep the walking route open</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Choose one option for the problem you actually have; do not fill every spare patch of floor.</li>
  <li>Leave room to open the ottoman and reach into the basket without moving another item first.</li>
  <li>Keep furniture away from door swings, heater clearances, and paths used at night.</li>
  <li>Use listed dimensions as a starting point, not a guaranteed tight fit. Recheck the exact model, color, pack count, seller, new or used condition, delivery estimate, and returns before ordering.</li>
</ul>
