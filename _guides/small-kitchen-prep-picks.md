<h2 id="choose-your-prep-task">Choose the task and its storage spot</h2>

<p>Keep useful tools you already own. Before adding something, measure the cabinet opening, the space above a shelf, or the clear height inside a closed drawer. Also check the working setup: a bowl on a scale takes more space than the scale alone, and a cutting board needs room around it for your hands and ingredients.</p>

<h2 id="weigh-in-an-existing-bowl">2. Weigh into a bowl you already use</h2>

<p>For recipes written by weight, a scale's tare function lets you subtract the bowl's weight and add ingredients in stages. That can avoid setting out several extra containers. The useful fit test is the scale with your chosen bowl on top: keep its base stable and the display visible.</p>

<h2 id="right-size-the-cutting-board">3. Use a small board for a small prep task</h2>

<p>A compact board is convenient for trimming a few vegetables or preparing a piece of fruit. It is not automatically the right size for a large batch. Choose a board that gives the food and your knife enough working room, rather than the smallest one that fits beside the sink.</p>

<h2 id="make-putting-away-easy">Make putting tools away as easy as taking them out</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Choose a nesting set only if its included tools will replace clutter, rather than duplicate it.</li>
  <li>Check the cabinet opening and drawer closure, not just the empty shelf or drawer base.</li>
  <li>Keep the scale protected from water and stored without weight pressing on its platform.</li>
  <li>Leave room for washed pieces to dry before nesting or enclosing them.</li>
  <li>Confirm the exact model, pack count, seller, item condition, delivery terms, and current availability before ordering.</li>
</ul>
