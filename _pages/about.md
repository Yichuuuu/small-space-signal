<p class="lede">Small Space Signal is an independent editorial project by Yichu. It helps renters and compact-home dwellers evaluate everyday products through dimensions, routines, maintenance, and real-world tradeoffs.</p>
<h2>Why this site exists</h2>

      <p>Small homes make ordinary buying mistakes expensive. A rack that folds to the wrong depth blocks a closet. A lamp with the wrong geometry creates screen glare. A storage bin that technically fits under the bed becomes useless when it cannot slide past the frame.</p>

      <p>Most buying pages begin with products. We begin with the room and the routine. Each guide identifies the constraint, explains the measurements that matter, compares common formats, and ends with a short decision checklist. The goal is not to recommend the most complicated item. It is to help a reader recognize the simplest item that will work.</p>


      <h2>How guides are developed</h2>

      <p>Articles are researched and written as original educational content. They are not copied from retailer descriptions or customer reviews. When a guide discusses a feature, it connects that feature to a practical outcome: easier cleaning, safer clearance, lower noise, faster access, or less damage at move-out.</p>

      <p>When hands-on product testing has not been performed, the article does not claim otherwise. Guides are framed as selection frameworks rather than invented reviews. Product specifications, prices, and availability can change, so readers should confirm current details with the manufacturer or retailer before purchasing.</p>


      <div class="checklist">
<h3>Editorial principles</h3>

<ul><li>Original analysis rather than rewritten listings.</li><li>Clear distinction between measured facts and editorial judgment.</li><li>No invented ownership, testing, or long-term-use claims.</li><li>No paid placement disguised as an independent recommendation.</li><li>Corrections are made when reliable new information changes a guide.</li></ul>
</aside>
